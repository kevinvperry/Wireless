# Deep Bass — 25 October 2026

Historical implementation record. The attenuation and binary toggle below are superseded by [Mid Bass and Audio Controls](MID_BASS_AUDIO_CONTROLS_2026-10-25.md).

## Behaviour

Audio Controls → Music now offers a **Deep Bass** toggle, off by default. The choice persists across stations and app launches. Audio Controls opens at full height to keep both sound presets accessible in the expanded form.

Deep Bass and Vocal Boost are alternative presets: enabling either switches the other off. This prevents Vocal Boost's 180 Hz high-pass filter from cancelling the bass shelf. TV Sync suspends both without clearing the saved choice. Ready, Waiting, Active and Unavailable reflect the shared processor's state; selecting Deep Bass on an unsupported stream does not claim active processing.

Listening Stats and History use a distinct `deepBass` mode only while processing is confirmed active. Ordinary or unsupported playback remains Live. JSON/CSV retain the mode through the existing generic persistence/export paths.

## Audio processing

Autohop was inspected before development. It has no bass preset; its existing connected effects graph, bypass controls and gain handling provide the implementation pattern. Wireless's existing shared direct-stream/HLS DSP now includes a low-shelf unit.

- Broad low shelf: 110 Hz, +5 dB.
- No speech high-pass or dynamics compressor in Deep Bass mode.
- Limiter pre-gain: −5 dB, reserving the shelf's full headroom; attack 2 ms and decay 50 ms.
- In-place preset changes on the enhanced engine retain its connected graph. When enabling bass, protective limiter gain is configured before the shelf.

This changes the tonal balance rather than reconstructing dynamics lost in the original broadcast. At fixed system volume, overall playback can sound quieter because of the reserved headroom. Adjust the listening volume to taste. Speakers, headphones and the station's processing determine the perceived result.

Compatible direct MP3/AAC streams use AVAudioEngine. Compatible HLS on iOS 27 uses the same native tap and unencrypted packed-AAC fallback as Vocal Boost. iOS 26 HLS, unsupported containers and protected audio report their limitations and continue ordinary playback. Native HLS tap execution remains unverified on hardware, as recorded in HLS_VOCAL_BOOST_2026-10-25.md.

## Validation

- Unit suite: 78 tests executed, 77 passed; the existing opt-in live-directory test skipped.
- Signal tests confirm a 3–6 dB low-frequency lift relative to the midrange at 44.1/48 kHz, retained low frequencies even with a conflicting speech-level argument, finite mono/stereo output, interleaved/non-interleaved PCM handling, and output peaks no greater than 1.01 for the tested near-full-scale bass signals. Existing Vocal Boost tests pass.
- A distinct Deep Bass mode round-trips through Codable.
- iOS 27 iPhone UI: Deep Bass/Vocal Boost exclusion in both directions, preference restoration after restart, and TV Sync suspension passed.
- iOS 27 iPad Pro 13-inch UI: availability, mutual exclusion, restart persistence and TV Sync suspension passed; screenshot inspected.
- iOS 26.5 iPhone UI: explicit HLS-unavailable state, preference persistence, preset switching and TV Sync suspension passed.
- Live packed-AAC HLS on the iOS 27 iPhone simulator: Deep Bass reached Active via the enhanced-engine fallback, and Off restored ordinary playback. The existing live HLS Vocal Boost test also passed.
- Unsigned generic iOS device build succeeded. No archive/upload was requested.

The in-app Support guide, SUPPORT_GUIDE.md and acknowledgements describe the new preset. Physical-device listening on rock/music stations, Bluetooth, AirPlay, background/interruption behaviour, and compatible Mac playback remain to be checked. Signal and simulator tests do not establish subjective bass quality or prove the native HLS tap runs on a physical device.

Follow-up: native HLS switching and cleanup have now passed a physical iPhone test on iOS 27.0.1.
